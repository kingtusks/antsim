Argentine Ant (Linepithema Humile)
Small - Worker 2-3 mm - Queen 4-6 mm
Reproduce very fast due to high queen count - The queens themselves do not reproduce faster compared to other species they simply just have more queens
Multiple queens per nest - Supercolony of nests can mean hundreds of queens
May temporarily farm aphids if they see them - Opportunistic farmers - Won’t bring the eggs back into the nest to survive winter and will instead just find new ones when warmer weather rolls around
Super colony - Nests never split and become aggressive with each other despite distance and lack of connection
If colony is split up by an enemy they will be unable to work together - But also if they are able to rejoin they will immediately work together again
Low genetic diversity in order to keep supercolonies also means low disease resistance
Adaptable nesting - Generally make shallow nests in loose leaf litter or beneath small stones or in debris - Are willing to take over a deeply dug abandoned nest they just suck at digging - If the nest shows any signs of not being water resistant they will move out due to flooding in natural habitat - Prefer moist moderate-warm temperatures but once again super adaptable in nesting
They don’t abandon their nests for no reason - However when they need to they are very efficient at both packing everything up and leaving but also at setting up their new nests - Nests are often temporary and will shift around with seasons - Prefer nests built under rocks in colder seasons to retain heat and then prefer nests built in tree bases or logs and with food sources nearby during warmer seasons
To establish a new nest one or multiple queens will walk to a nearby location to establish the new nest - During this they will be accompanied by workers as bodyguards and to speed the establishment of the new colony - Once again they walk above ground to the new location
Will send ants to fight at and extend their border and form new nests constantly and endlessly unlike most ants which attack in raids usually lasting only a couple hours
Ants that find food sources, enemy nests, potential nests to expand into will begin to lay down a scent trail and head back to the nest - They then recruit more ants and they follow the scent trail back - Nests within the colony are also connected by these trails - If a trail goes unused for too long it will lose its scent and be lost - Nests will often send ants to other nests of the same colony to make sure the scent trail is not lost - Even if trail is lost they will not become enemy colonies they just won’t remember how to get to each other
Border advancement constant but slow - Will attempt to harass and attack lone or small groups rather than full scale invasion on the nest
War of attrition strategy - Will attempt to steal food to dwindle enemy forces - Will try to establish nests close to the enemy to further outcompete them
If a nest is destroyed queens and workers will attempt to flee to nearby nests in their colony - Those new nests will just take them in and they will just begin working or laying eggs like usual
Very large colony in California supposedly has 1 trillion ants - Generally about 1 queen for every 125 ants
Aggressive ant - Highly competitive - If they see another ant species at a food source they will often swarm them and try to steal the food and drive the other species away
A nest having some queens die often leads to the remaining queen(s) ramping up their reproduction rates - I  believe this is due to there being more resources to go around with one of the queens dying
Fight by swarming and tearing apart opponents through group effort - No soldier caste so they’re all able to defend themselves - They curl their abdomen forward and spray a toxin that causes irritation and disorientation which makes it easier to swarm - Overall the toxin is really not that strong though - The toxin will also alarm and attract nearby colony allies to the battle
Nests will become more queen dense in winter - Once winter passes some of the queens will leave to expand territory
Food warehouse room is rather small - Prefer to keep food in a special expandable stomach pouch - This means they just keep the food on them instead of leave it somewhere in the nest - They’re also willing to share food mouth to mouth
Don’t ever hibernate - During winter egg production and activity will slow in order to save resources - However they will still wait for just a warm winter day and peak heat hours to scavenge a bit - When it’s really cold they’ll just chill in the nest packed together to preserve heat
Nests will have multiple entrance holes
Lack of consistent nest layout
Queens don’t have a specified royal chamber - Queens and workers just be all over - Queens tend to stay near the eggs though
Omnivore opportunistic species - Will eat whatever is convenient and easy
Young colonies will prioritize protein in order to grow and reproduce quickly - Older colonies will prioritize honeydew to fuel and feed the established population
Spring - Queens lay a lot of eggs - High protein demand
Summer - Egg laying isn’t biggest priority - High honeydew and fruit demand to feed everyone - Colony is at peak population
Fall - Colony begins preparing to reduce activity - Are less picky about food in this stage
Winter - Don’t have lots of time and energy to scavenge - Will prefer honeydew or fruit - Just maintain the population throughout winter
In late winter egg production will ramp to prepare for and be at a high population for spring
Queens will still lay eggs year round the amount just fluctuates - Due to this they constantly need the mixed diet of both protein and honeydew/sugar
